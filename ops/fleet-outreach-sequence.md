# Fleet Outreach Sequence — B&W Auto Repair LLC

Cold outreach to fleet decision-makers in Middlesex and Mercer counties. Four touches over
about three weeks, then stop. Everything here is written to be sent from a real person at
the shop, not from "marketing."

**Send from:** a named human at B&W, not `info@`. A fleet manager replies to people.
**Reply-to:** an inbox someone actually watches — `bw_autorepair@hotmail.com` or better, a
new `service@bwautorepairllc.com` once the domain is set up.
**Landing page:** `bwautorepairllc.com/fleet`

---

## Before you send anything

**1. References stay anonymous.** The emails below say *"two public school districts in
central New Jersey"* rather than naming anyone. That is accurate, it is verifiable if someone
asks, and it costs you nothing to send today.

There is a stronger version — naming the districts — but a customer's name is theirs to give,
not yours to use. If you ever want it, it is two phone calls: *"Would you be comfortable if we
mentioned that we service your vehicles when we talk to other districts?"* Most say yes. Until
then, send the anonymous version as written; it works.

**2. Build the list by hand, not by scraping.** Fleet outreach lives or dies on reaching the
right title. For each target you want: organization, contact name, title, email, phone.

| Segment | Title to look for | Where to find them |
|---|---|---|
| School districts | Transportation Supervisor / Director of Facilities | District website, "Departments" |
| Municipal / township | DPW Superintendent / Fleet Manager | Town website, public directory |
| Service-van businesses | Owner / Operations Manager | Google Maps, company site |
| Houses of worship | Facilities or Operations contact | Congregation website |
| Trucking / delivery | Owner / Safety Manager | Company site, DOT records |
| Auto dealers | Service Manager / Fixed Ops Director | Dealership site |

**3. Fifty good contacts beat five hundred guesses.** A short list you researched converts;
a long list you bought gets deleted and hurts your sending domain.

**4. One caution on tooling.** If you run this through Instantly, keep it on a separate
sending domain from `bwautorepairllc.com` — a domain used for cold outreach should never be
the one your customers' estimates and invoices come from. Warm the new domain before sending
volume.

## Touch 1 — Day 0

The whole job of this email is to get read in the four seconds before it's deleted. Short,
specific, one question.

**Subject line options** — test them, don't guess:
- `Vehicle service for {{ORGANIZATION}}`
- `Question about your {{VEHICLE_TYPE}}`
- `Fleet service in Monroe Township`

```
Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

I own B&W Auto Repair in Monroe Township — we've been on Route 33 for over
fifteen years.

We do a lot of fleet work: vans, pickups, box trucks, and buses. Diesel and
medium-duty included, which is the part most independent shops send out. We
also tow, so when a vehicle goes down we recover it and it's in our bay the
same day rather than waiting on somebody else's truck.

Would it be worth me looking at one of your vehicles? No charge — I'll tell
you what I see and what it would cost, and you can compare it to whoever you
use now.

{{YOUR_NAME}}
B&W Auto Repair LLC
609.362.5476 | bwautorepairllc.com/fleet
```

**Stronger opener, still anonymous** — use this when the recipient is a school district:

```
I own B&W Auto Repair in Monroe Township. We already service the vehicle fleets
for two public school districts in central New Jersey, and I'm reaching out
because we have capacity for another account this year.
```

Note the second sentence does real work: *capacity for another account* says you are
selective and currently busy, which is both true and the opposite of how a struggling shop
sounds.

## Touch 2 — Day 4

Not "just following up." Give them something they didn't have.

**Subject:** `Re: {{TOUCH_1_SUBJECT}}` — keep it on the same thread.

```
Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

One thing I should have mentioned: the reason fleets end up with us is usually
downtime, not price.

A vehicle that breaks down Tuesday and gets towed by one company, diagnosed
Wednesday, and repaired the following week costs far more in lost days than
the repair itself. We tow it, diagnose it, and repair it under one roof —
usually same or next day.

If you've got a vehicle that's been sitting or one that keeps coming back for
the same problem, that's the one I'd like to look at.

{{YOUR_NAME}}
609.362.5476
```

## Touch 3 — Day 11

Switch the ask. They didn't want a meeting; offer something smaller.

**Subject:** `Worth keeping our number?`

```
Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

I'll stop filling your inbox — but one practical offer before I do.

Keep our number for the day a vehicle goes down and your usual shop can't take
it for a week. We tow, and we work on diesel and medium-duty. No account or
paperwork needed to call.

609.362.5476 — 843 Route 33 West, Monroe Township.

If it's more useful for me to check back at budget time instead, tell me when
and I'll make a note.

{{YOUR_NAME}}
```

The question at the end is the point. "Check back in June" is a win, not a no.

## Touch 4 — Day 21 — close the loop

**Subject:** `Closing this out`

```
Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

I haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing isn't right and leave you
alone.

If that changes — a vehicle down, a shop that let you down, or a budget cycle
where you're rebidding — we're on Route 33 and the number is 609.362.5476.

Thanks for your time.

{{YOUR_NAME}}
```

Genuinely stop here. A clean exit leaves the door open; a fifth email closes it.

## If they reply

**"Send me information."** Don't send a brochure. Reply with two sentences and the fleet
page link, then ask for the vehicle: *"Here's the overview — bwautorepairllc.com/fleet.
The fastest way to see if we're a fit is one vehicle. What's giving you the most trouble
right now?"*

**"What are your rates?"** Answer plainly, and quote the *commercial* rate — a fleet manager
expects a dedicated rate below retail and will think less of you if you don't have one. What
you must not do is discount off a rate that is already below market. See
`ops/labor-rate-research.md`: at $125/hr B&W is under the New Jersey average of $140–170, so
there is nothing left to give. Set retail at $145–155 first, hold commercial at $125–135, and
quote diesel and medium-duty higher. Then compete on downtime and capability, not price.

**"We're happy with who we use."** Ask one question: *"Understood. Do they handle your
diesel and medium-duty in-house, or send it out?"* If the answer is "send it out," you have
a wedge. If not, thank them and move on.

**"Yes, come look."** This is the whole point of the sequence. Go the same week, look at the
vehicle, write the estimate on the spot, and send it through QuickBooks so they get the PDF
and the approve button. Use the estimate templates in `ops/estimate-templates.md`.

## Phone script — for the follow-up call

Call the ones who opened but didn't reply. Two days after Touch 2.

```
"Hi, is this {{FIRST_NAME}}? This is {{YOUR_NAME}} from B&W Auto Repair over
in Monroe Township — I sent you a note about fleet service, I don't know if it
reached you.

I'll be quick. We work on fleet vehicles including diesel and medium-duty, and
we tow our own. I'm not trying to move your whole account — I'd just like to
look at one vehicle, free, so you know we exist for the day you need us.

Is there one that's been giving you trouble?"
```

If they're busy: *"No problem — when's a better time?"* Then call then. Actually call then.

## Tracking

Keep it in a spreadsheet, not in your head. One row per contact:

`Organization · Contact · Title · Email · Phone · Segment · Touch 1 · Touch 2 · Touch 3 ·
Touch 4 · Replied · Outcome · Next action date`

**What good looks like on a researched list of 50:** a handful of replies, one or two
vehicle reviews booked, and one account opened. One school district is worth roughly
$20,000 a year based on what your largest district account has spent. That is the math that makes
this worth the hours.
