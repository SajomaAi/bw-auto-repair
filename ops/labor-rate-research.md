# Labor Rate Research — Fleet & Commercial Pricing

Researched August 2026. Sources listed at the bottom. The short version: the question
"what discount do shops give fleets" turns out to be the wrong question for B&W.

---

## 1. Where the market sits

| | Rate per hour |
|---|---|
| US average, independent shops (2026) | **~$132** (range $120–159; lowest state $85, highest $197) |
| **New Jersey (2026)** | **$140 – $170** |
| Dealerships | $20–40/hr above nearby independents |
| Diesel / truck repair, most shops | $120 – $175 |
| Diesel median, national | $135 (2024) → **$149 (2025)**, +7.4% year over year |
| NY / Philadelphia metro truck rates | $180 – $200 |
| **B&W Auto Repair, current** | **$125** |

North Jersey tracks the New York metro; South Jersey tracks Philadelphia. Monroe Township
sits between the two.

---

## 2. What shops normally charge fleets

Fleet discounting is standard practice, not an exception. The common structure is a
dedicated commercial rate — below retail, and the same every visit rather than negotiated
job by job.

**Typical volume tiers:**

| Fleet size | Discount off retail |
|---|---|
| 5 – 10 vehicles | 5% |
| 11 – 25 vehicles | 10% |
| 26 – 50 vehicles | 15% |
| 51+ vehicles | 18 – 20% |

Those tiers assume the shop still holds roughly **25–30% gross margin** on commercial work.
Pre-negotiated fleet pricing generally lands **15–25% below** walk-in or emergency rates.

Deeper discounts are expected to buy something in return: guaranteed monthly volume, a
multi-year commitment, or both.

---

## 3. The finding that matters for B&W

**B&W is at $125/hr in a state that averages $140–170. The fleet discount has already been
given — to everybody.**

Work it through. A normally-priced NJ shop at $155 retail, giving a 15-vehicle fleet the
standard 10%, bills that fleet **$139.50**. B&W bills *everyone* — fleets, walk-ins,
one-time customers who will never return — **$125**.

That means:

- B&W's retail rate is already below the *discounted commercial rate* of a correctly priced
  competitor.
- There is no room left to discount for a fleet, because the discount is already spent.
- Retail customers are subsidizing nothing, because there is nothing to subsidize — the
  whole book is underpriced.
- Worst of all, a fleet manager comparing quotes sees a number so far below market it can
  read as inexperience rather than value.

**Every billed hour is leaving roughly $20–30 on the table.**

---

## 4. Recommended structure

**Step 1 — raise the retail rate to $145–155.**
Mid-range for New Jersey, still comfortably under the dealers. This is the move that
matters; everything else follows from it.

**Step 2 — set the commercial rate at $125–135.**
Today's rate becomes the fleet rate. Fleet accounts receive a real, defensible discount off
a real retail number, and B&W stops selling retail work at fleet pricing. Existing fleet
customers see no increase at the bottom of that band, which makes this the least disruptive
version of a price rise.

**Step 3 — price diesel and medium-duty separately, at $150–165.**
This work is genuinely scarcer, the market pays more for it (median $149 nationally and
climbing 7%+ a year), and B&W is one of the few independents in the area that will take it.
Charging light-duty auto rates for medium-duty truck work is the single clearest place money
is being left behind.

**Step 4 — make discounts buy something.**
Below the commercial rate, ask for a volume commitment or a term. A discount given for
nothing is just a lower price.

---

## 5. Two guardrails from the research

**Keep the effective rate within 10% of posted.** Effective labor rate is what you actually
collect per billed hour after discounts, comebacks, and unbilled diagnostic time. Industry
guidance: if posted is $147, effective should not fall below about $132. Anything wider means
discounts, unbilled diag, or undercharging on complex jobs are eating the rate.

**Watch the payment terms.** Small commercial accounts typically pay net 30; larger
contracts run net 60 to net 90. Public entities are often slowest. Weigh whether the volume
justifies the wait before agreeing to extended terms — and note that B&W currently has
**$8,970 in invoices showing unpaid**, $4,035 of it from one district account. Terms discipline
matters as much as rate discipline.

---

## 6. What this corrects

An earlier draft of `ops/fleet-outreach-sequence.md` advised *"Don't discount to win a
fleet."* That was too absolute. A commercial rate is normal and a fleet manager will expect
one; refusing outright reads as inflexible.

The accurate version: **discount from a proper retail rate, never from an already-low one.**
B&W's problem was never that its fleet discount was too generous — it was that no retail rate
existed to discount from.
